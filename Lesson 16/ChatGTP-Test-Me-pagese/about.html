About Global Warming

A clear introduction to what global warming means, why it is happening, what it changes and how people can respond.

The basic idea

Global warming and climate change are related, but not identical.

Global warming is the long-term heating of Earth’s surface. Climate change is the broader term for warming and the many connected changes it causes, including sea-level rise, ice loss and shifting weather patterns.

The greenhouse effect is natural and keeps Earth warm enough for life. The problem is that human activities have added extra greenhouse gases, causing more heat to remain in the climate system.

Main causes

Where greenhouse gas emissions come from

Many activities release carbon dioxide, methane and other heat-trapping gases.

Energy and industry

Burning coal, oil and gas for electricity, heating and manufacturing releases large amounts of carbon dioxide.

Transportation

Cars, trucks, ships and planes often use fossil fuels, producing greenhouse gas emissions.

Land-use change

Cutting forests releases stored carbon and removes trees that could absorb carbon dioxide.

Effects

Warming changes more than temperature.

  • Heatwaves can become more frequent and intense.
  • Ice loss contributes to rising sea levels.
  • Rainfall patterns can shift, increasing drought or flooding risks.
  • Plants, animals and ecosystems may struggle to adapt quickly.
  • Communities can face risks to health, food, water and infrastructure.
Solutions

Climate action includes both mitigation and adaptation.

Mitigation means reducing greenhouse gas emissions or increasing carbon storage. Adaptation means preparing communities and ecosystems for climate impacts that are already happening or cannot be fully avoided.

  • Use renewable electricity and improve energy efficiency.
  • Choose cleaner transport and design walkable communities.
  • Protect forests, wetlands, soils and oceans.
  • Reduce waste and use materials more carefully.
  • Build infrastructure that can handle heat, floods and other risks.
